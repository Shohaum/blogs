title: "Why Real Users Teach You More Than Personal Projects"
date: "2026-10-08"
tags:

---

Personal projects are a great way to learn.

You choose the problem, define the features, decide the architecture, and set your own deadlines. You can experiment freely and build exactly what you have in mind.

But there is a boundary to that learning: **you are still the one defining the problem.**

When you build something for yourself, you naturally make decisions within the boundaries of what you already understand. You might brainstorm dozens of features, think about scaling, handle edge cases, and design the architecture carefully, but ultimately, you are predicting what might happen.

Real users don't let you predict.

They tell you what actually happens.

A real product starts changing the moment people depend on it. Someone will ask for a feature you never considered. Someone will use your system in a way you didn't expect. Someone will find an edge case you never thought was possible. Something that worked perfectly for ten users might become a problem at a thousand.

And suddenly, "I didn't think users would do that" isn't an excuse.

You have to investigate. Research. Redesign. Debug. Sometimes throw away your original approach and find a completely different solution.

**That is where a different kind of growth happens.**

You start learning not just how to build software, but how to build software that survives reality.

You learn to deal with incomplete requirements, changing priorities, unexpected failures, performance bottlenecks, backward compatibility, usability, testing, monitoring, and most importantly feedback.

A personal project can teach you how to build.

**Real users teach you what it means to build for someone other than yourself.**

That's why I believe a small project with ten real users can sometimes teach you more than a project with a hundred carefully planned features that nobody uses.

The real challenge isn't building something that works.

**It's building something that keeps working when people start depending on it.**
